What are fjords

A fjord is a deep, narrow inlet of the sea with steep cliffs or mountains on each side. They are formed when glaciers carve out valleys during the Ice Age, and later, when the glaciers melt, seawater floods these valleys. Fjords are typically found in regions with a history of glaciation, such as Norway, New Zealand, and parts of Canada. They are known for their stunning natural beauty, with dramatic landscapes that often include waterfalls, lush vegetation, and unique wildlife. Fjords are popular tourist destinations and offer opportunities for activities like hiking, kayaking, and boat tours.

Shoreline

Norwegian fjords stretch along dramatic shorelines where forests meet the sea. Their calm waters reflect towering cliffs, creating a landscape that feels both peaceful and powerful.!

Misty

Fjords often appear mysterious when covered in mist, creating a dramatic atmosphere where sea and mountains meet. This natural scenery reflects Norway’s unique coastal beauty.

Mountains

Norwegian fjords are surrounded by steep mountains carved by ancient glaciers. Their towering cliffs and deep waters make them one of the world’s most breathtaking landscapes.
